Autoscalers.bias_correction — Method
bias_correction(samples, estimate)

The bias correction $z_0 = Φ^{-1}(P(θ^* < \hat{θ}))$ of the BC interval (Efron, 1987), counting ties as half. The fraction is kept within $1/(2n)$ of 0 and 1, so that $z_0$ stays finite.

julia> Autoscalers.bias_correction(1:9, 5) == 0  # centred: 4.5 of 9 below
true

julia> round(Autoscalers.bias_correction(1:9, 7); digits=3)  # 6.5 of 9 below
0.589

julia> round(Autoscalers.bias_correction(1:9, 100); digits=3)  # clamped at 1 - 1/18
1.593

Autoscalers.error_dual — Method
error_dual(tag, value, (slot, errors), i, ::Val{K})

value as a dual number whose slot-th of K partials is its error. Propagated through the ansatz, the partials of the scaled $y$ are then $σ_c \, ∂y/∂c$ for each column $c$ with errors, and their norm is the first-order error of the scaled $y$.

Autoscalers.extrapolate — Method
extrapolate(xs, ys, dys, x)

Like interpolate(xs, ys, dys, x; sq_errs=true), but defined everywhere. Returns (y, dy2, d). Outside the data:

  • y continues linearly from the end segment;
  • dy2 is held at its value at the end;
  • d is the distance outside the data range, in units of the range width (0 inside).
julia> xs, ys, dys = [0.0, 1.0, 2.0], [0.0, 10.0, 20.0], [1.0, 1.0, 3.0];

julia> Autoscalers.extrapolate(xs, ys, dys, 1.5)
(15.0, 5.0, 0.0)

julia> Autoscalers.extrapolate(xs, ys, dys, 3.0)  # half a range width beyond the end
(30.0, 9.0, 0.5)

Autoscalers.fixed_window_breakdown — Method
fixed_window_breakdown(a::Autoscaler, params)

Fixed-window cost, split into the collapse (χ²-like) part and the extrapolation penalty. Each curve contributes the mean over its scored points; the result is averaged over curves with at least one scored point.

Autoscalers.interpolate — Method
interpolate(x, y, y_err, selected_x; sq_errs=false)

Linearly interpolate y and y_err between the knots x at selected_x. With sq_errs=true, the squared errors are interpolated and returned instead. Returns (y, y_err), or missing outside the range of x.

julia> x, y, y_err = [0.0, 1.0, 2.0], [0.0, 10.0, 20.0], [1.0, 1.0, 3.0];

julia> Autoscalers.interpolate(x, y, y_err, 1.5)
(15.0, 2.0)

julia> Autoscalers.interpolate(x, y, y_err, 1.5; sq_errs=true)  # mean of 1² and 3²
(15.0, 5.0)

julia> Autoscalers.interpolate(x, y, y_err, 3.0)
missing

Autoscalers.model_data — Method
model_data(a::Autoscaler, params; maxiter=50)

Data that lies exactly on the fitted collapse at the free parameters params. For each data point, the value in the first column with errors is replaced by the value whose scaled y equals the pooled_master_curve at that point. The ansatz is solved for it with Newton's method, which needs a single step when the scaled y is linear in that column.

Autoscalers.select_subset — Method
select_subset(c::Autoscaler, selected_x, selected_curve)

From every curve other than selected_curve, select the two neighbouring points $j$ and $j + 1$ with $x_j ≤ x_\mathrm{sel} ≤ x_{j+1}$, where $x_\mathrm{sel}$ is selected_x, or nothing if that curve does not bracket it (Houdayer & Hartmann (2004), Appendix A).

source
Autoscalers.standard_cost_function — Method
standard_cost_function(c::Autoscaler, params)

Quality $S$ of Eq. A1: the mean of $(y - Y)^2 / (δy^2 + δY^2)$ over all points for which the master curve $Y ± δY$ is defined. Inf if it is defined for none.

References

  • J. Houdayer and A. K. Hartmann, Low-temperature behavior of two-dimensional Gaussian Ising spin glasses, Phys. Rev. B 70, 014418 (2004), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.70.014418.
