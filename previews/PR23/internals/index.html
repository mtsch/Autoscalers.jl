Autoscalers.bias_correction — Method
bias_correction(samples, estimate)

The bias correction $z_0 = Φ^{-1}(P(θ^* < \hat{θ}))$ of the BC interval (Efron, 1987), counting ties as half. The fraction is kept within $1/(2n)$ of 0 and 1, so that $z_0$ stays finite.

julia> Autoscalers.bias_correction(1:9, 5) == 0  # centred: 4.5 of 9 below
true

julia> round(Autoscalers.bias_correction(1:9, 7); digits=3)  # 6.5 of 9 below
0.589

julia> round(Autoscalers.bias_correction(1:9, 100); digits=3)  # clamped at 1 - 1/18
1.593

Autoscalers.bracket — Method
bracket(x, selected_x)

The index $j$ of the two neighbouring points $j$ and $j + 1$ of a curve with $x_j ≤ x_\mathrm{sel} ≤ x_{j+1}$, where $x_\mathrm{sel}$ is selected_x, or nothing if the curve does not bracket it (Houdayer & Hartmann (2004), Appendix A).

Autoscalers.error_dual — Method
error_dual(tag, value, (slot, errors), i, ::Val{K})

value as a dual number whose slot-th of K partials is its error. Propagated through the ansatz, the partials of the scaled $y$ are then $σ_c \, ∂y/∂c$ for each column $c$ with errors, and their norm is the first-order error of the scaled $y$.

Autoscalers.extrapolate — Method
extrapolate(xs, ys, dys, x; exact=false)

Like interpolate(xs, ys, dys, x; exact), but defined everywhere. Outside the data, y continues linearly from the end segment and the variance is held at its value at the end.

julia> xs, ys, dys = [0.0, 1.0, 2.0], [0.0, 10.0, 20.0], [1.0, 1.0, 3.0];

julia> Autoscalers.extrapolate(xs, ys, dys, 1.5)
(15.0, 5.0)

julia> Autoscalers.extrapolate(xs, ys, dys, 3.0)
(30.0, 9.0)

Autoscalers.extrapolation_free_window — Method
extrapolation_free_window(a::Autoscaler, args)

At the free parameters args, the largest part (lo, hi) of eval_window in which the window cost needs no extrapolation, or nothing if there is none. Always nothing for the classic cost, which has no window.

Each curve that the window cost uses is covered by all other curves for scaled x between the largest first and the smallest last scaled x of the others. That range is mapped back to the curve's unscaled x, and these intervals are intersected.

Autoscalers.fixed_master_curve — Method
fixed_master_curve(a::Autoscaler, x, own_curve; exact=false)

Error-weighted master curve at scaled x, built from all curves except own_curve (unless a.use_all_sizes), extrapolating where needed. Returns (; Y, dY2). exact selects the variance of each curve's interpolant, as in extrapolate.

Autoscalers.interpolate — Method
interpolate(x, y, y_err, selected_x; exact=false)
interpolate(x, y, selected_x)

Linearly interpolate y between the knots x at selected_x, and return (y, variance), or missing outside the range of x. The variance interpolates y_err² linearly or, with exact=true, is the variance of the interpolant of independent points, $(1 - λ)^2 σ_k^2 + λ^2 σ_{k+1}^2$ with $λ = (x_\mathrm{sel} - x_k)/(x_{k+1} - x_k)$. Without y_err, only the interpolated y is returned.

julia> x, y, y_err = [0.0, 1.0, 2.0], [0.0, 10.0, 20.0], [1.0, 1.0, 3.0];

julia> Autoscalers.interpolate(x, y, y_err, 1.5)  # mean of 1² and 3²
(15.0, 5.0)

julia> Autoscalers.interpolate(x, y, y_err, 1.5; exact=true)  # (1² + 3²) / 4
(15.0, 2.5)

julia> Autoscalers.interpolate(x, y, 1.5)
15.0

julia> Autoscalers.interpolate(x, y, y_err, 3.0)
missing

Autoscalers.model_data — Method
model_data(a::Autoscaler, params; maxiter=50)

Data that lies exactly on the fitted collapse at the free parameters params. For each data point, the value in the first column with errors is replaced by the value whose scaled y equals the pooled_master_curve at that point. The ansatz is solved for it with Newton's method, which needs a single step when the scaled y is linear in that column.

Autoscalers.standard_cost_function — Method
standard_cost_function(c::Autoscaler, params)

Quality $S$ of Eq. A1: the mean of $(y - Y)^2 / (δy^2 + δY^2)$ over all points for which the master curve $Y ± δY$ is defined. Inf if it is defined for none.
